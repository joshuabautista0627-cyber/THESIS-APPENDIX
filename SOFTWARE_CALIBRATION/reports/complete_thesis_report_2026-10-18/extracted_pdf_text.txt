1. Purpose, system and dataset
The study estimates applied force from optical measurements, detects contact, and identifies the contacted taxel in a
fixed 3 × 3 array. It compares model families under a common grouped validation design and evaluates improvements
from richer optical features, hyperparameter search, causal history, threshold selection and ensembles.
The SPARE application records a camera stream alongside an Arduino Nano/HX711 reference-force stream, unloaded
optical baselines, timestamps and nine regions of interest (ROIs). Reference force provides supervision and evaluation
targets; it is absent from optical model inputs. The separate printer-driven automatic-calibration path is excluded.
Historical hardware records identify an Arducam IMX179 module, but do not establish identical camera controls or a
traceable force-uncertainty budget for every archived recording. [S4, S5, S15]

3. Feature construction and preprocessing
Training-only normalization
For each taxel, the fitted positive-light floor is the median of the within-recording medians of positive-light sums in
training no-contact recordings. The positive feature is max(raw positive sum − floor, 0) / ROI area. Signed light is
divided by area without clipping. Floors are refitted at each inner and outer training boundary. [S5, S8, S9]
Weighted StandardScaler uses training rows only. For feature k, z_k = (x_k − μ_k) / σ_k, with weighted mean and
population variance fitted on training data. Centering is disabled only for the positive-only force pack; variance scaling
remains enabled. Zero-variance scale follows the library implementation. The selected best models all use centered
and scaled features.

Across the nine taxels, unit(x) = x / (Σ|x| + 10⁻⁶), and contrast(x) = x − median(x). The seven pattern blocks are
unit(positive), unit(contrast(signed)), unit(active), contrast(signed), contrast(active), unit(contrast(raw V mean)), and
unit(contrast(signed median)).
Spatial adds signed median, signed MAD, normalized centroid x and y, centroid-presence flags, contrast(raw V mean),
contrast(raw V median), raw V maximum / (raw V mean + 1), log(1 + positive), and sign(signed)·log(1 + |signed|).
Invariant contains the seven pattern blocks plus unit(MAD), centroid x/y, presence, unit(contrast(raw V median)), and
the maximum-to-mean ratio. Its name does not prove invariance to all illumination or geometry changes.
Causal temporal history
The optical pack supplies fast and slow exponential moving averages (EMAs), with time constants 0.3 s and 1.0 s.
Temporal concatenates spatial, fast, slow, optical − fast, and fast − slow. At positive time step Δt, α = 1 − exp(−Δt/τ) and
h_t = h_(t−1) + α(x_t − h_(t−1)). History restarts on a new recording, nonpositive Δt, or a gap greater than 0.3 s. Output
smoothing is a separate selection over τ   {0, 0.3, 1.0} s.
Inputs contain no reference force, true taxel, TEST number, session identity, absolute time or recording duration.
Identity and relative timestamps only partition/reset history and supply time differences. The exact ordered
feature-construction function is printed in Appendix C.

4. Alignment, validation and metric definitions
Reference-force alignment
At optical time t, the target is reference force at t − L. Linear interpolation remains within a recording; duplicate
reference timestamps are reduced by their median. Extrapolation and interpolation over gaps greater than 300 ms are
prohibited. Lag candidates are 0, 100, 200, 300, 400 and 500 ms. Inner grouped NNLS MAE selects the smallest lag
within 0.5% of the best error. TEST1–TEST6 use outer lags 300, 300, 200, 300, 300 and 300 ms, respectively. Tuning
reuses these fold-local alignment decisions to retain identical evaluated rows and targets. [S4, S5]
Nested whole-group evaluation
Each outer fold holds out one complete TEST group and its assigned no-contact recordings. The other five groups form
five inner folds: four groups fit, one validates. Preprocessing, hyperparameters, feature pack, history, correction,
thresholds and model choice are determined without the outer group. Fifteen possible four-group fits are cached; only
the relevant inner-validation group is consumed for an outer selection. Thirty inner train/validation boundaries were
audited. The final research refit uses all 15 leave-two-groups-out splits for selection, then fits the full pool. [S5, S9]
Earlier optical rows from a held-out recording can initialize causal history, even if their force lies outside the scored
interval. Their labels are not consumed. These are chronological stream predictions at stationary per-recording target
taxels, not independent cold-start frames or demonstrated moving-contact localization.
One weighting convention for reported results
For an evaluated recording s with n_s eligible rows, each row receives weight w_i = 1/n_s. Each recording therefore
contributes one total unit. All headline, comparison and confusion-matrix percentages use these weights. For a
force-bin subgroup, n_s is recomputed using only that bin. Raw matrix counts are deliberately unweighted integers.
Training may use additional class/bin balancing; that is separate from evaluation weighting.

5. Models, search and improvement strategy
The fixed benchmark evaluates nine force regressors and six classifiers for each classification task. Seed 1729, 22
training subsets and 21 task-specific estimators yield 462 fits. Force uses the positive 9-feature pack; classifiers use
optical 27. Force fitting balances recordings and 0.25 N bins, localization balances recordings, and contact balances
classes and recordings. Fixed models use native decision rules without temporal smoothing, threshold tuning or
force-output clipping. [S4, S7]
The tuning run uses seed 1741 and a finite grid of 178 estimator/feature configurations: 62 force, 64 localization and
52 contact. Fifteen four-group training subsets yield 2,670 inner estimator fits. Additional output-history, correction,
threshold and ensemble variants reuse cached inner predictions and are not counted as additional grid candidates.
Appendix A lists every candidate, not only the winning settings. [S5, S8]

The tuned selector chooses across families using inner MAE, localization macro F1 or contact balanced accuracy; it
adopts an ensemble only if the inner criterion improves. The tuned family rows instead restrict that inner search to one
family. The present headline ranking follows the requested highest classification accuracy and lowest force MAE across
the evaluated outer results.
SVC sigmoid/softmax transformations provide score representations, not calibrated probabilities. A 1.0 s EMA time
constant does not prove a measured 1.0 s event delay. The FPR ≤5% condition is an inner-selection constraint, not a
guaranteed outer FPR.

10.3 Raw V against reference force for every taxel
The following plots pair the pressed taxel's raw ROI mean HSV V with its existing lag-aligned reference force. All 7,154
eligible press frames from the 54 manual recordings are retained. Each taxel contains six TEST recordings. No-contact
recordings are excluded because this analysis examines measured press-force variation; they are not assigned artificial

removal is applied. [S17]
The x-axis is aligned reference force F in newtons, and the y-axis is raw ROI mean V in the 0-255 digital encoding. All
nine panels share identical limits, with the V axis focused on the observed brightness range rather than spanning the
full encoding. Colors and marker shapes identify TEST1-TEST6. Each point is a recorded frame; point density reflects
raw sample counts, while fitted lines and pooled statistics give each recording equal total weight.
Correlation and fitted-line definitions
For a recording with n_s eligible frames, w_i = 1/n_s. Pearson r is the weighted covariance of F and V divided by the
square root of their weighted variances, centered at their weighted means. The line V_hat = a + bF minimizes Σw_i(V_i
- a - bF_i)². R² = 1 - Σw_i(V_i - V_hat_i)² / Σw_i(V_i - weighted mean V)². With this single-predictor, intercept-inclusive fit
and the same weights, R² equals r²; both are reported because r retains direction.
These are descriptive fits on the plotted samples. The samples coincide with the earlier held-out frame identities, but
the new straight lines are fitted and summarized on those same samples. Their R² values describe raw-V linear
association and must not be presented as held-out accuracy, prospective validation, or the force ensemble's R² of
0.3113.

Findings for the thesis

0.099412 at T3, so the straight-line fit accounts for approximately 9.94% of its weighted raw-V variance. A raw mean V

future calibration study. They do not prove a physical sensitivity, a linear optical transfer function, or a specific cause of
brightness offsets. The fitted slope has units of camera V levels/N and is descriptive of this dataset, not a traceable
sensitivity calibration.

optical patterns and causal history to predict force, so its held-out force R² cannot be equated with the R² of a
raw-V-against-force line. The existing best-model metrics and all competing-model comparisons remain unchanged.

What normalization can and cannot change
Within a recording, V₀ is a fixed positive constant. Subtracting it or dividing by it is an affine transformation, so Pearson
r and an intercept-inclusive linear-fit R² are unchanged. This was numerically checked for all 54 recordings.
Consequently, the within-recording tables in Section 10.3 also apply to both transformed signals. Their slopes,
intercepts and units do change.
Across pooled recordings, different V₀ values change relative offsets and scales, so pooled r and R² can change.
Reporting V/V₀ instead of 100 × (V - V₀)/V₀ would yield exactly the same r and R² because these two representations
are related by a single positive affine transformation. Dividing every raw value by 255 alone would also leave its raw r
and R² unchanged.

12. Findings, limitations and next experiments
Findings supported by the measurements
Expanded optical patterns, causal history, hyperparameter tuning and output policies improve the pooled best results
over the fixed benchmark. The most accurate localization family is regularized Logistic regression, the most accurate
contact family is RBF SVC, and the lowest-MAE force model is a three-family ensemble. Increased model complexity
alone is not an explanation: tree and MLP classifiers do not lead localization accuracy.
The improvement is measured for the combined development strategy. The run does not contain a full factorial
ablation that isolates the causal contribution of each feature, correction, weighting choice or model. Differences
between the frame-only selector and the best family suggest useful temporal information, but are not a controlled
estimate of a single EMA effect.
Contact detection is consistently strong within the defined press/control comparison. Localization errors concentrate
in TEST1 and specific taxel confusions, especially T2. Force regression remains the weakest task: overall R² is 0.3113
and low-force errors are large. Optical distributions show measurable off-target response, but classifiers can use
distributed patterns successfully.
Raw-V pooled fits have R² below 0.10 for all nine taxels. Saved-baseline subtraction and normalization improve pooled
R² for eight taxels; normalized T3 reaches r = 0.5501 and R² = 0.302639. T1 remains weak and its R² decreases.
Within-recording correlations are unchanged by these affine transformations. These descriptive findings support
baseline adjustment across recordings, while independent calibration validation remains necessary.
Limits that must remain in the thesis
The dataset contains one archived manual collection with six TEST groups and 11 no-contact controls, not an
independent multi-device or multi-day prospective trial. Family ranking uses observed outer results, so winner
selection can be optimistic. Temporal smoothing benefits stationary recorded contacts; moving and multiple
simultaneous contacts were not evaluated. Contact prevalence, dedicated-control design and restricted force eligibility
limit transfer to continuous deployment.
Reference-force uncertainty, physical taxel coordinates in millimetres, calibrated illumination, controlled
matched-force sweeps, onset/release annotations and deployment latency were not established in this analysis.
Accordingly, this report does not claim millimetre localization error, lux response, force detection limit, hysteresis,
full-scale linearity, dynamic response time or a validated operating range.
Specific next experiments
Freeze the three headline model families and all preprocessing before collecting a new manual test dataset. Repeat
complete sessions across days, camera settings and mechanical reassemblies; include continuous no-contact periods,
moving contacts and controlled onset/release transitions. Keep new test recordings outside all threshold and
hyperparameter selection.
For force, collect balanced repeated loading/unloading plateaus near 0.05–1 N and throughout 1–3 N; preserve loading
direction and synchronized timing. For localization, prioritize T2 and the TEST1 acquisition conditions and record
matched-force contacts to separate lighting spread from force effects. For contact, evaluate false alarms per unit time
and missed events once episode annotations exist. Benchmark the exact intended winner stack with camera
acquisition and feature extraction included before making latency claims.

Appendix C. Exact feature-construction source
The following function preserves the executed block order. AREA_COLUMNS, RAW_LIGHT_COLUMNS, SIGNED and
ACTIVE_COLUMNS list the nine taxels in ascending order; they respectively contain ROI areas, raw positive-light sums,
signed-light sums and active fractions. Frames must already be ordered chronologically within recording. The input
floor is the training-only nine-vector described in Section 3.
def causal_ema(values, session_ids, times, tau):
    """Only current/past rows; restart at session boundary or >0.3 s gap."""
    if tau == 0:
        return np.asarray(values).copy()
    values = np.asarray(values)
    result = np.empty_like(values, dtype=float)
    for i in range(len(values)):
        dt = times[i] - times[i-1] if i else 0
        if i == 0 or session_ids[i] != session_ids[i-1] or dt > 0.3 or dt <= 0:
            result[i] = values[i]
        else:
            a = -np.expm1(-dt / tau)
            result[i] = result[i-1] + a * (values[i] - result[i-1])
    return result
def optical_features(frames, floors):
    """Return fixed optical feature packs; labels/force/absolute time are absent."""
    def cols(s): return frames[[f"roi{r}_{s}" for r in range(1,10)]].to_numpy(float)
    area = frames[AREA_COLUMNS].to_numpy(float)
    if not (np.isfinite(area).all() and (area > 0).all()):
        raise ValueError("Invalid ROI areas")
    positive = np.maximum(frames[RAW_LIGHT_COLUMNS].to_numpy(float) - floors, 0) / area
    signed = frames[SIGNED].to_numpy(float) / area
    active = frames[ACTIVE_COLUMNS].to_numpy(float)
    optical = np.column_stack([positive, signed, active])
    if not np.isfinite(optical).all(): raise ValueError("Nonfinite basic optical features")
    def unit(x): return x / (np.sum(np.abs(x), axis=1, keepdims=True) + 1e-6)
    def contrast(x): return x - np.median(x, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    med, mad = cols("signed_delta_v_median"), cols("signed_delta_v_mad")
    raw = cols("raw_v_mean")
    rawmed, rawmax = cols("raw_v_median"), cols("raw_v_max")
    cx = (cols("centroid_x") - cols("x")) / np.maximum(cols("width"), 1)
    cy = (cols("centroid_y") - cols("y")) / np.maximum(cols("height"), 1)
    present = (np.isfinite(cx) & np.isfinite(cy)).astype(float)
    cx, cy = np.nan_to_num(cx, nan=0.5), np.nan_to_num(cy, nan=0.5)
    shape = np.column_stack([unit(positive), unit(contrast(signed)), unit(active),
        contrast(signed), contrast(active), unit(contrast(raw)), unit(contrast(med))])
    spatial = np.column_stack([optical, shape, med, mad, cx, cy, present,
        contrast(raw), contrast(rawmed), rawmax / (raw + 1),
        np.log1p(positive), np.sign(signed) * np.log1p(np.abs(signed))])
    invariant = np.column_stack([shape, unit(mad), cx, cy, present,
        unit(contrast(rawmed)), rawmax / (raw + 1)])
    ids = frames.session_id.to_numpy()
    times = frames.capture_monotonic_relative_s.to_numpy(float)
    fast = causal_ema(optical, ids, times, .3)
    slow = causal_ema(optical, ids, times, 1.0)
    temporal = np.column_stack([spatial, fast, slow, optical-fast, fast-slow])
    packs = dict(positive=positive, optical=optical,
        shape=np.column_stack([optical, shape]), spatial=spatial,
        invariant=invariant, temporal=temporal)
    for name, x in packs.items():
        if not np.isfinite(x).all(): raise ValueError(f"Nonfinite {name} features")
    return packs
The feature code computes histories for all available optical frames before eligibility masking. Neither the target force
nor target taxel enters the estimator feature array. No neural-network segmentation, image morphology, future-frame
filtering or zero-response rejection is applied.
